The Science of System Persistence

Every system in the universe—whether a biological cell, a national economy, a software platform, or a galaxy—constantly faces natural breakdown and disorder. Triadmics Science is an interdisciplinary framework designed to answer one central question: Why do some systems continue to exist over time, while others collapse?

The Odero Axiom
$$C(I) \cdot E > D$$
A system persists if and only if its Information-Directed Control ($C(I)$) multiplied by its Available Energy ($E$) outweighs its System Decay ($D$).

Breaking Down the Root Equation

To keep any system stable and functioning, three core elements are constantly interacting:

  • Information-Directed Control ($C(I)$): This represents the instructions, rules, and feedback mechanisms that tell the system how to organize itself. Raw energy alone is useless without correct guidance—$C(I)$ is the intelligence directing where energy must go.
  • Available Usable Energy ($E$): The physical or operational fuel required to perform work, repair damage, and execute tasks.
  • System Decay ($D$): The natural background wear-and-tear, friction, noise, or entropy pushing the system toward collapse.

The Persistence Capacity ($P$): The combined strength of a system's control and energy is defined as $P = C(I) \cdot E$.

The Persistence Index ($\Omega$): We measure systemic health using the ratio $\Omega = \frac{P}{D}$.

  • If $\Omega > 1$, the system grows, repairs itself, and maintains stability.
  • If $\Omega = 1$, the system is in a delicate balance.
  • If $\Omega < 1$, the system enters structural failure and eventual collapse.
