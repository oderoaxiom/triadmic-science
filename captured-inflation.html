Theory of Captured Inflation

Applying Persistence Science to Macroeconomic Systems

In standard economic theory, when supply chain disruptions increase costs, prices rise. However, when supply chains recover, prices rarely return to their original lower levels. The Theory of Captured Inflation uses the Odero Axiom to model why consumer prices remain elevated even after production costs drop.

Key Core Concepts

  • Asymmetric Price Transmission: When input costs rise, firms rapidly pass costs to consumers to preserve their persistence index ($\Omega$). When input costs drop, market power allows firms to "capture" the difference as margin expansion rather than lowering prices.
  • The Odero Capture Gap: Measures the structural gap between falling producer costs and sticky consumer prices.
  • Systemic Control Breakdown: Demonstrates that perpetual cost-of-living increases represent a decay of consumer purchasing control ($C(I)$), eroding broader economic stability over time.
