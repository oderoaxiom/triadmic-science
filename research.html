Theory of Autonomous Control Collapse Ongoing

Agentic Systems, Control Degradation, and Infrastructure Persistence

As software architecture transitions from passive, static large language models to autonomous agentic loops capable of executing real-world infrastructure operations, execution velocity may exceed human or automated oversight.

Ω = 1

Extending the domain-invariant Odero Axiom, C(I) × E > D, this paper proposes the Theory of Autonomous Control Collapse. The theory holds that agentic-system stability requires persistence capacity to remain greater than internal error, control, and operational decay. When computational throughput increases rapidly while alignment-control efficiency deteriorates through recursive error amplification, delayed feedback, or governance bottlenecks, the system may approach the critical persistence boundary Ω = 1 and subsequently enter decay dominance at Ω < 1.

  • Agentic execution velocity
  • Alignment-control efficiency
  • Recursive error degradation
  • Oversight and correction bottlenecks
  • Cascading infrastructure failure
  • Autonomous-system persistence thresholds
  • Recovery and safe-interruption mechanisms
