System Dynamics & Control Theory

Continuous State-Space Formulation of the Odero Persistence Axiom

Applied physics within Persistence Science models structural continuity using continuous state-space formulations. By analyzing the temporal evolution of the Persistence Index ($\Omega$), system velocity ($\omega$), acceleration ($\alpha$), and control-phase geometry ($\kappa$), we can rigorously evaluate how complex mechanical and energetic systems maintain stability or undergo systemic collapse.

Dynamic Equilibrium Formulation
$$\frac{d\Omega}{dt} = \omega(t) - \gamma D(t)$$
Tracking the rate of change of structural persistence relative to active control velocity ($\omega$) and environmental decay dissipation ($\gamma D$).

Kinematic Vectors & Equations

The applied physics framework expands the primary axiom into explicit kinematic state-space equations:

Omega Velocity ($\omega$)
$$\omega(t) = \frac{d\Omega}{dt}$$
The temporal rate of change of structural persistence capacity.
Persistence Acceleration ($\alpha$)
$$\alpha(t) = \frac{d\omega}{dt} = \frac{d^2\Omega}{dt^2}$$
The second derivative tracking compounding or exhausting adaptation.
  • Control-Phase Simplex ($\kappa$): The multi-dimensional geometric boundary defining stable operational regimes vs structural failure states.
