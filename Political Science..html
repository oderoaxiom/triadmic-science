The Persistence of Power

A Coalition Indispensability Theory of Democratic Leader Longevity

Did you know that executive survival isn't just about political luck? By applying the Odero Axiom to democratic political systems, this framework reveals how leader longevity is strictly governed by the Political Persistence Index and Political Omega Velocity.

Core Political Theory

Coalition Indispensability Theory

Proposes that democratic leaders survive not through unlimited resources, but because coalition indispensability ($\kappa$) acts as an internal structural decay dampener.

Core Postulates:
  • Persistence Condition: Leaders maintain office if political control efficiency acting on resource throughput exceeds adjusted decay ($\Omega_{pol} > 1.0$).
  • Decay Dampening: Indispensability ($\kappa$) lowers effective political decay ($D_{pol\_adjusted} = D_{pol} / \kappa$).
$$\Omega_{pol} = \frac{\kappa \cdot C_{pol}(I)E_{pol}}{D_{pol}}$$

Political Persistence Index incorporating control efficiency ($C_{pol}(I)$), energy throughput ($E_{pol}$), and indispensability ($\kappa$).

Dynamic Early-Warning Metric

Political Omega Velocity ($\omega_{pol}$)

Measures the rate of change of the persistence index over time, providing rigorous early warning for executive collapse before margin breach.

Key Takeaway:
  • Leaders survive because $\Omega_{pol} > 1$, but leaders endure because $\omega_{pol} > 0$.
  • Negative velocity ($\omega_{pol} < 0$) strongly forecasts executive collapse prior to margin breach ($\Omega_{pol} \le 1.0$).
$$\omega_{pol} = \frac{d\Omega_{pol}}{dt} = \Omega_{pol}\left[\frac{\dot{C}_{pol}}{C_{pol}} + \frac{\dot{E}_{pol}}{E_{pol}} + \frac{\dot{\kappa}}{\kappa} - \frac{\dot{D}_{pol}}{D_{pol}}\right]$$

Dynamic velocity equation balancing control, resource growth, indispensability shift, and decay acceleration.

How Leaders Survive: Comparing Political Theories

Instead of looking at politics through complicated math and jargon, our theory explains why political leaders stay in power by comparing how they build coalitions, handle daily pressures, and control government systems.

  • How We Compare to Traditional Ideas: Older theories focus mainly on how leaders share money and perks with their inner circle to stay in power. Our approach looks at something simpler: *How hard would it be to replace this leader?* (replacement cost) and *How fast is their political strength growing or shrinking?*
  • The Three Types of Government Styles: By looking at how much a government tries to steer things versus how much energy it actually uses, we can sort countries into three simple groups:
    • Strict Autocracies/Tyrannies: Systems where leaders focus entirely on tight control with very little room for public pushback.
    • Stable Democracies: Balanced systems where leadership and public systems work in a steady, ongoing rhythm.
    • Gridlocked Governments: Systems where so many different groups are steering at once that the government slows down and gets stuck.
  • Everyday Political Tools: This approach breaks down how leaders actually manage day-to-day survival—looking at how much influence they hold, how well they resist political wear-and-tear, how efficiently they get things done, and how they handle political risks.
